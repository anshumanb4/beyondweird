# Indigenous AI Project — Summary

## The Big Idea

Current AI models reason using a narrow, Western-centric logic — prioritizing efficiency, cost-benefit analysis, and individual optimization. Instead of simply "de-biasing" these models, this project proposes **re-biasing** them: building AI systems that reason using Indigenous knowledge systems grounded in relationality, land kinship, seven-generation responsibility, and circular thinking.

The argument is not that Western reasoning is wrong, but that limiting AI to one way of thinking constrains innovation — especially in fields like sustainability, governance, and education where relational, long-term thinking is desperately needed.

## The Main Project: A One-Day Conference (May 2026)

Neva is not fixated on any single technical approach to building Indigenous wisdom-informed AI models. The right next step is to **organize a convening** — a one-day conference in May 2026 that brings together people from different aspects of this problem for panel discussions and collaborative conversation.

The conference will bring together voices from:

- Indigenous knowledge holders and community leaders
- AI researchers and builders
- Data sovereignty and Indigenous data governance experts
- Epistemologists and scholars of Indigenous ways of knowing
- Designers and systems thinkers
- Policy and sustainability practitioners

The goal is to spark the key conversations that need to happen before any model-building begins — around ethics, consent, governance, and what "Indigenous-aligned AI" should even look like.

## Background & Context

### Why Indigenous Reasoning?

Indigenous reasoning systems are not just "alternative values" — they represent structurally different architectures of thinking. Key characteristics include:

- **Relationality** — knowledge arises from relationships among people, nature, and the spiritual world
- **Holism** — physical, emotional, spiritual, and intellectual dimensions are inseparable
- **Oral transmission** — knowledge preserved through storytelling, ceremony, and living memory
- **Place-based learning** — knowledge tied to specific landscapes and local contexts
- **Seven Generation Principle** — decisions should sustain the world seven generations into the future

### The Scaffolding Approach

Before any model gets built, the project proposes building ethical and technical infrastructure:

1. **Testimony collection** — hundreds of conversations with Indigenous knowledge-holders about real decisions
2. **Reasoning labels** — tagging excerpts with descriptors like "kinship-based reasoning" or "long-term thinking," reviewed by community members
3. **Consent and governance protocols** — grounded in CARE Principles and Indigenous data sovereignty
4. **New evaluation methods** — community-reviewed alignment rather than standard AI benchmarks

### Technical Approaches Under Consideration

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Role-play prompting | Zero setup, quick sanity checks | Relies on base model's thin data; creates stereotypes |
| ValuesRAG-style retrieval | Keeps raw testimonies outside the model; citations; easy to update | Needs good tagging & retrieval logic |
| Fine-tuning / LoRA | Nuance baked into weights; works offline | Higher cost; harder to audit/remove content |

The project is not committed to any one approach — that's part of what the conference is meant to help figure out.

### Use Cases

- Educational curriculum design
- Climate solutions and environmental governance
- Urban planning and construction
- Economic and investment policy
- Cultural storytelling and preservation

### Key Ethical Principles

- Start with relationships, not data
- Communal consent (ongoing and revocable)
- Communities define their own benefit
- Do not flatten difference
- Major decisions involve communities
- Work in public — nothing released without community review

### Existing Research & Frameworks Referenced

- CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance
- OCAP (First Nations data sovereignty)
- ValuesRAG cultural alignment methodology
- Donella Meadows' Leverage Points (intervening at the paradigm level)
- Two-Eyed Seeing (Mi'kmaq framework)
- UNDRIP (UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples)
